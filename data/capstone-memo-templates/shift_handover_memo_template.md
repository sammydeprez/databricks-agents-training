# Shift Handover Memo Template

**Fictional company procedure, for training use only.**

Every outgoing shift lead completes this memo before leaving site. It is not optional, even on a
quiet shift — "nothing to report" is itself a valid, required entry.

## Template

Shift: [Day / Night]
Date: [Date]
Outgoing shift lead: [Name]
Incoming shift lead: [Name]

**Open work orders handed over:** [list with priority, or "none"]
**Equipment currently isolated or under maintenance:** [list, or "none"]
**Safety observations this shift:** [list, or "none"]
**Anything the incoming lead should watch for:** [free text, or "none"]

## Process

1. The outgoing shift lead completes the memo and reviews it face to face with the incoming shift
   lead — a written memo alone is not a valid handover, both must be present.
2. Any open High priority work order must be explicitly acknowledged by the incoming lead, verbally
   and in writing on the memo, before the outgoing lead can leave site.
3. If no incoming lead is available at shift end (short-staffing, delay), the outgoing lead escalates
   to the duty manager, who either extends the outgoing lead's shift or personally receives the
   handover. The memo is never simply left on a desk without a recipient.
4. Completed memos are filed in the shift log system within 30 minutes of shift end and are kept for
   90 days.

## Common questions

- **What happens if the incoming shift lead disagrees with something in the memo?** They note the
  disagreement directly on the memo before filing it — the memo is not edited or reissued after
  handover, since it's the record of what was actually communicated at the time.
- **Does a "quiet shift" with nothing to report still need a memo?** Yes — file it with all sections
  marked "none," it is still required for the 90-day record.
